**Can, check one thing before posting:** the drafts call the method "learning
by sound, in code-length order, at full character speed with long gaps"
(what the app does). If the method you actually learned has a name or a
source (a book, an instructor, Koch, Farnsworth, a club), say it in tweet 3.
Credit makes the story stronger and more credible. Don't add anything that
didn't happen: no number of weeks, no failed attempts you didn't have.
Tweet 2 says you started by memorising the chart. That's inferred from
"mors ezberlemem zorunluydu"; delete it if it isn't how it went.

What the story can and can't say:
- ✅ You're taking an amateur sailing course that requires an amateur radio
  certificate; you restarted that training, and Morse was required for you.
- ✅ Confirmed: this is the requirement in Turkey. The English copy says "here
  in Turkey" so nobody reads it as a universal rule.
- ✅ You're still in training, so not licensed yet. That's also why operators'
  corrections are welcome.
- ❌ Don't generalise "Morse is required for an amateur licence". Rules differ
  by country and class. Keep it personal: "benim için zorunluydu" / "I had to
  learn Morse".

## X thread (English)

**1/** (video: `video/renders/morseforge-promo-short-9x16-en.mp4`, 0:57)

> I'm learning to sail here in Turkey, and my course requires an amateur radio
> certificate.
>
> So I finally restarted the radio training I'd put off for years, and it came
> with one requirement for me: Morse code.
>
> I turned the way I learned it into an interactive app. 🧵

**2/**

> My first attempt was everyone's: memorise the chart. A = dot dash, B = dash
> dot dot dot…
>
> The catch is that a chart teaches you to *translate*. You hear beeps, turn
> them into dots and dashes, then look up the letter. That extra step doesn't
> go away with practice.

**3/**

> What worked was learning each letter by its sound.
>
> Shortest codes first (E is one dot, T is one dash). Characters at full speed,
> gaps stretched out. You stop counting beeps and start hearing letters.

**4/** (image: `assets/01-hero-first-signal-1080x1350.png`)

> In MorseForge the front page is the first lesson.
>
> Tap the key once: you've sent E. Hold it: T.
>
> Two characters on day one, all 26 letters and 10 digits by day seven.

**5/** (image: `assets/03-telegraph-sos-1080x1350.png`)

> Your keyboard becomes a brass telegraph key, decoded as you send.
>
> Some words answer back. Try SOS.

**6/** (image: `assets/04-challenge-link-1080x1350.png`)

> Finish the 60-second test and you get a link. Your friend gets the same
> characters, in the same order, at the same speed, so your scores actually
> compare.
>
> There's also a daily that's the same for everyone.

**7/**

> No account, no ads, works offline. Lamp mode if you'd rather learn with the
> sound off. Free and open source.
>
> morse.iamcanturk.dev

**8/**

> I'm still in training, not licensed yet. If you're an operator and something
> on-air is off, please tell me. Those are the replies I want most. 73

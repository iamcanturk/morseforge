## Post (English)

> I'm taking an amateur sailing course in Turkey, and it requires an amateur radio
> certificate. So I restarted the radio training I'd put off for a long time,
> and for me it came with a requirement: Morse code.
>
> The usual way is a chart. But memorising that A is dot-dash teaches you to
> *translate*: hear the beeps, turn them into dots and dashes, look the letter
> up. That lookup step is a fixed cost, and practice doesn't remove it. What
> worked for me was learning each letter by its sound.
>
> So I turned that method into an interactive trainer, and made its front page
> the first lesson.
> Tap the key once and you've sent E. Every character after that is seen,
> heard, and sent back with your own keyboard: two on day one, all 26 letters
> and 10 digits by day seven.
>
> A few product decisions I'd make again:
> → A challenge link carries the same characters in the same order, so two
>   people's scores actually compare.
> → The daily share shows squares and a percentage, never the answers.
> → Every listening drill works with the sound off, as a lamp.
> → No account, no ads, works offline. Progress stays on the device.
>
> It's free and open source: morse.iamcanturk.dev
>
> #MorseCode #WebDevelopment #OpenSource
